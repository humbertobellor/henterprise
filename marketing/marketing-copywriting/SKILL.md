# Marketing copywriting

Copy for any surface, and the editing passes that make it work.
`enterprise/marketing/brand-voice` is the upstream dependency — draft before it exists and it comes
back sounding like everyone else.

## When to Use

- Writing or rewriting page copy.
- Tightening a draft.
- Fixing copy that is vague or feature-led.
- Adapting one message across formats.
- Reviewing copy before it ships.

## Procedure

### Before writing a word

Settle four things. Copy written without them is decoration:

- **Who** is reading, and what they already believe.
- **What one thing** they should understand afterward.
- **What they should do**, singular.
- **What is stopping them** — the actual objection, not the polite version.

### Writing

**Lead with the consequence, not the mechanism.** People buy what a thing lets them do. The feature
is evidence for the claim, not the claim.

**Specific beats superlative, always.** "Cuts invoice processing from three days to twenty minutes"
outperforms "dramatically faster" because it can be believed and checked. Every superlative is a
claim your competitor also makes.

**Write the way the reader talks.** Their words, from research or support tickets, not internal
vocabulary. If a phrase only exists inside the company, it does not go on the page.

**One idea per section, in the reader's order** — problem they recognize, why existing options fail,
what you do differently, proof, what to do next. Not your org chart's order.

**Every claim carries proof** nearby: a number, a name, a screenshot, a quote. A page of unbacked
claims reads as marketing and is discounted wholesale.

### Editing

Edit in passes; doing them at once does none of them.

1. **Structure** — is the order right, and is anything missing or duplicated? Fix this before
   sentences.
2. **Cut** — remove every sentence that does not advance the argument. Then cut the first
   paragraph, which is usually warm-up.
3. **Sharpen** — replace abstractions with specifics, passive with active, hedges with claims.
4. **Read aloud** — anything you stumble over gets rewritten. This catches what silent reading does
   not.

## Pitfalls

Kill on sight: empty openers ("In today's fast-paced world"), we-focused framing where you-focused
works, hedges that weaken a true claim, feature lists with no consequence attached, and any adjective
a competitor could equally use.

And structurally: writing before the four questions are settled, leading with the mechanism rather
than the consequence, using internal vocabulary on a customer-facing page, and running the editing
passes simultaneously, which does none of them.

## Verification

Read it aloud — the pass that catches what silent reading does not. Then check every claim has proof
within sight of it, that the page states one action, and that no sentence survives which does not
advance the argument. If the copy could be lifted onto a competitor's site unchanged, it says nothing.
