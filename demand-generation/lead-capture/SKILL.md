# Lead capture

Turning anonymous traffic into known contacts, and deciding when that trade is worth making at all.

## When to Use

- Designing or improving a lead magnet.
- Planning a free tool.
- Adding or fixing popups and overlays.
- Improving form conversion.
- Deciding what is worth gating and what should stay open.

## Procedure

### What gating costs

Every gate trades reach for contacts. That trade is worth making only when the contact is genuinely
worth more than the reader — which is true far less often than it is assumed.

Gate: things with immediate practical utility that a buyer would use during evaluation — templates,
calculators, assessments, data. Do not gate: thought leadership, anything you want cited or shared,
or anything a competitor publishes openly.

### Lead magnets

The test is whether someone would pay a small amount for it. If not, it will not earn an email
address that gets used.

The best ones are things people *use* rather than read: a template that saves an afternoon, a
calculator that answers a specific question, a checklist for a process they are mid-way through.
An ebook that restates blog posts is not one.

Match the magnet to a stage. Someone downloading a beginner's guide is not ready for a sales call,
and treating them as if they are burns the list.

### Free tools

The strongest form: durable, earns links, and qualifies by use rather than by claim.

Build one only if it does something genuinely useful standalone, and keep the gate light — let
people use it, and ask for the email to save or export the result. Gating before value is delivered
converts a fraction as well.

### Popups and overlays

Popups work and are widely hated; both facts matter.

- **Trigger on behavior, not time** — exit intent, scroll depth, or a second visit. A popup at three
  seconds interrupts someone who has not yet decided whether to care.
- **Once per visitor**, remembered. Repeated interruption is what generates the hatred.
- **Never on mobile mid-content.** Beyond the user cost, it is penalized.
- **Offer something specific.** "Subscribe to our newsletter" is not an offer. "The pricing
  calculator" is.
- **One clear dismissal.** Hidden or tiny close buttons cost more in reputation than they gain in
  conversions.

### Forms

Every field costs conversion. Ask for what you need to do the next step and nothing for a later one
— enrichment can fill the rest. Explain any field whose purpose is not obvious, since unexplained
fields are where people abandon.

## Pitfalls

- Gating thought leadership, or anything you want cited or shared.
- A lead magnet nobody would pay a small amount for.
- Gating a free tool before any value is delivered.
- Popups triggered on time rather than behavior, repeated per visit, or shown mid-content on mobile.
- Hidden or tiny dismissal controls.
- Form fields collected for a later step, which cost conversion now.
- Passing an early-stage download to sales as if it were intent.

## Verification

Judge a gate by what it cost, not only by what it collected: compare reach and downstream
qualification against the ungated baseline for the same asset. Check that each captured contact
enters a sequence matched to its stage — a beginner's-guide download routed to a sales call burns
the list. For forms, confirm every field is needed for the *next* step and that anything non-obvious
is explained.
